Engineering is not about accumulating tools; it is about developing the patience to observe where things break.

A reflection on beginning my undergraduate journey at BGS College of Engineering and Technology, learning the fundamentals of computation, and applying human-centered design through IDT.

When I entered BGS College of Engineering and Technology, I expected engineering to begin with complex formulas and high-level abstractions. What I quickly realized during my first semester is that great engineering begins far more humbly: with disciplined observation, careful listening, and the humility to examine mundane friction.

My coursework in Innovation and Design Thinking (IDT) fundamentally shifted my perspective on what it means to build. Rather than jumping straight into code or wiring sensors together, IDT forced us to walk around campus, speak with maintenance staff, observe how students navigate lecture halls, and draft empathy maps before writing a single line of logic. That balance—rigorous human understanding paired with mathematical and computational discipline—is what I find most rewarding.

"A system is only as good as its behavior in the hands of someone who is tired, distracted, or in a rush. If the design fails them, the engineering has failed."

Current Technical Inquiries

Right now, my days are divided between two complementary modes of problem-solving. In our computer science and programming labs, I spend hours stepping through pointers, memory allocations, and basic algorithms in ANSI C. Writing C gives me an appreciation for the physical machine beneath every abstraction—how registers store numbers, how the stack expands, and why memory hygiene matters.

In our IDT labs and design workshops, the machine is replaced by people. We study user feedback loops, dissect why public utilities in Bengaluru face adoption hurdles, and sketch rapid prototypes using inexpensive materials before committing to code. It is an honest, iterative way to learn.

Engineering Aspirations

Over the next three years at BGSCET, my goal is to build deep proficiency in systems engineering, embedded programming, and accessible interface design. I want to build tools that solve genuine domestic and civic needs in Karnataka—whether that means agricultural sensor networks, resilient public transit trackers, or efficient campus software tools that make daily administrative tasks effortless.

Core Working Principles

A few personal standards I hold myself accountable to across coursework, lab reports, and team collaborations.

01 / Empathy First

Never prescribe a technical solution before understanding the person who will maintain or operate it.

02 / Ruthless Clarity

Prefer simple, transparent logic over clever code that obscures bugs or creates maintenance debt.

03 / Build to Learn

Don't linger in theoretical planning. Build a rough low-fidelity prototype early to let reality test your assumptions.
